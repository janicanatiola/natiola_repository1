Three easy-care plants for a brighter room

Meet three unfussy plants that bring a little life to a bright corner.

Read the story

Start with a pothos, a snake plant, or a sturdy rubber plant. Give each a spot with the right light, let the soil dry a little between waterings, and check in before adding more water. A simple routine is better than fussing every day.

A small-space guide to growing your own greens

Fresh herbs and salad leaves can thrive in a few well-chosen containers.

Read the story

Choose a container with drainage, place it where it gets several hours of light, and start with quick-growing greens like lettuce or arugula. Keep the soil evenly moist and harvest a few outer leaves at a time so the plant can keep growing.

Slow living   /   3 min read

Make a weekend ritual out of plant care

A slower look at your plants can turn a small chore into a reset.

Read the story

Put on some music, check the soil, wipe dusty leaves, and turn pots toward the light. Notice new growth and remove anything that has dried out. A few calm minutes each week help you spot changes before they become problems.

Plant care   /   4 min read

How to know when your plant needs water

Skip the calendar and let your plant and its soil tell you what it needs.

Read the story

Touch the top few centimeters of soil before watering. If it still feels damp, check again later. When it is dry, water slowly until a little drains from the bottom, then empty the saucer. The timing changes with the season, light, and pot size.

In the garden   /   6 min read

Turn your balcony into a tiny garden

Use the light, the walls, and a few sturdy pots to make the most of your space.

Read the story

Notice where the sun falls throughout the day before choosing plants. Use rail planters only where they are permitted and securely fitted, add vertical shelves for smaller pots, and group plants with similar watering needs together. Start small so the space stays easy to care for.

Slow living   /   5 min read

Let the seasons guide your growing

A seasonal rhythm makes it easier to work with nature instead of against it.

Read the story

As days get shorter, many houseplants grow more slowly and need less water. In spring, fresh growth may call for a little more light and room. Pay attention to what changes in your home through the year and adjust your care gradually.
